# Bounded decoded texture retention candidate (.22)

This candidate changes eviction of CPU-decoded PVRTC textures. It is based on `.21` and keeps the configured total payload and entry limits. It requires gameplay validation before release promotion.

A new inexpensive texture starts in a probationary group. A texture with a measured or learned decode cost of at least 2 ms, or one that receives a cache hit, enters a retained group. The retained group is limited to three-quarters of both configured bytes and entries: at 512 MiB and 16,384 entries, at most 384 MiB and 12,288 entries. These are caps, not preallocated reservations; either group can initially use available total space.

When the retained group exceeds a cap, its least recently used entry moves to the front of probation without dropping its pixels. Eviction prefers the oldest probationary entry. Larger admissions can still evict retained content when necessary to satisfy the total bounds. A single texture larger than the retained byte cap stays probationary. Preparation preserves the profile's priority order within the bounded retained group; demand admissions and hits update recency. Expensive newly encountered textures and reused content can therefore displace old-map preparation. There is no game or track whitelist, permanent pin or additional decoded-pixel copy.

This policy aims to reduce expensive re-decoding after transient loading activity. It does not predict unseen textures, prevent all evictions, preload GPU resources or eliminate JIT/pipeline work. It may trade cheap re-decoding for retaining more expensive content. Map transitions and games with different reuse patterns must be tested. This is different from the rejected `.12` preload-list reversal.

The existing **Settings → Texture Decoding → Decoded Texture Cache** control enables this behavior with a nonzero budget; no extra toggle is needed. The tooltip describes bounded retention. Optional texture telemetry appends `RetainedBytes`, `RetainedEntries`, `RetentionPromotions` and `RetentionDemotions` to the existing schema-2 CSV. Existing coverage reports still identify prepared content decoded after eviction. Payload limits exclude bookkeeping, learning history and decode scratch memory; the intrusive queues add two links and a flag per bounded cache entry, with no queue allocation on hits.

Validation includes byte/result identity, cheap one-pass loading streams, ranked preparation, promotion on reuse, expensive new-map replacement, oversized admissions, independent byte/entry caps, clear/budget changes and concurrent cache tests. The real PVRTC decoder/profile reload tests must remain byte-identical. Synthetic tests establish mechanics, not a measured game-performance improvement. Use exact matching source/EXE/PDB provenance and the same frozen learning inputs for `.21`/`.22` comparison; keep raw game-derived profiles private.
